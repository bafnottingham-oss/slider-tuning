Tips

  • Work over metalRest it on a metal tray before lifting a body half; the magnets cling to the metal instead of jumping out.
  • Detent slot faces one wayFit it with the narrow edge facing outward.
  • Lift magnets cleanlyA magnet puller or holder makes re-tuning far easier. Keep small parts in a dish.
  • Limit screw, in four moves1. Start the screw, stopping just below the back surface. 2. Drop in spring, brass plunger, ceramic ball; push the strip in. 3. Two full turns. 4. Slide the strip back and forth to check it's held.
  • Better less than moreOvertightening a limit screw locks the strip solid and can damage the body.
  • Keep the track cleanGrit scratches it. Blu Tack lifts debris; isopropyl alcohol for metal parts.
  • Magnet strengthEvery magnet in the kit is N52, so strength changes come from size and how many you fit. A 5x6 pulls harder than the stock 5x4, so the slider hits the end harder and snaps home faster.
  • Magnet safetyStrong N52 magnets and balls. Keep away from young children.

Card notes

  • Right part, right holeSeat each part in its own hole. Don't force anything, or you can damage the body.
  • Wear is normalBeads rubbing the plate wear over time. Clean out bead residue now and then and reassemble to keep the feel at its best.

Know your parts

  • 8 side and 6 sideFancy play sliders have one card with 8 magnets and one with 6. On the Aura Sand the 8 side is always the steel card; the 6 side is always the black Teflon card.
  • Halfmoon spacer vs halfmoon padThe spacers are the thin plastic crescents on the sprue; cut them out and file off any excess so they sit flat in the cassette. The pads are the separate black discs. Most setups here use the spacer, not the pad.
  • Long halfmoon vs long halfmoon padThe long pieces sit with the middle magnets. Swapping the pad for the plain long halfmoon on the 6 side gives more crunch. The pad is very slightly weaker.
  • 0.2 mm metal spacerSits under the corner magnet, at the bottom of the pocket.
  • Foam padsLeaving them out gives more crunch and, for some, more stability and flow, because the magnets can move a little. One on the 8 side is the middle ground.
  • Getting magnets outUse a metal tool, such as an Allen key, or a magnet to lift them out of the cassette.
  • Keep the plates cleanDebris caught between the plates damages them, especially the Teflon side. Blu Tack lifts grit.

Quick guide

Magnet strength, and what you'll feel

The N number is the magnet's grade: how much magnetic energy it can hold. Higher is stronger for the same size. N52 is about 7% stronger at the surface than N45, and the pull between two magnets goes with the square of that, so roughly 15% more pull.

ChangePullWhen you slide the 6 side
N52 instead of N45 (same size)About 15% strongerFirmer holds and a sharper snap into each spot. Harder to slip off in fast tricks, but a little more effort to move.
N45 instead of N52About 15% weakerSofter, floatier glide that's easier to move. Slips off sooner when you add power or speed.
Thicker magnet (8 × 2.5 mm vs 8 × 2 mm)StrongerFirmer, crunchier holds.
More gap between the magnets (pads, plastic spacers, foam)Weaker, and drops off fast as the gap growsSofter, quieter holds. Even a thin pad makes a difference you can feel.

Grade, size and gap all add up, so a weaker grade can be balanced by a thicker magnet or a smaller gap. Standard N magnets lose strength for good above about 80 °C, so keep the slider off hot dashboards and radiators.

Unofficial. Strength ratings are relative, based on how the setups compare with each other.
